THE BASICS

Types of contraceptive pills

Oral contraceptives are not all the same. The two main categories differ in the hormones they contain.

01

Combined oral contraceptive pill

This type contains two hormones: estrogen and progestin. It mainly works by preventing ovulation. It can also thicken cervical mucus, making it harder for sperm to reach an egg.

  • May make periods more regular, lighter, or less painful.
  • May not be suitable for everyone, including some people with a history of blood clots, certain migraine types, or particular cardiovascular risks.
  • Taking it consistently as directed is important for effectiveness.
02

Progestin-only pill (POP)

This pill contains progestin but no estrogen. Depending on the formulation, it works by thickening cervical mucus and may also prevent ovulation.

  • May be an option for some people who cannot use estrogen.
  • Bleeding patterns may change, including spotting or periods that become irregular or stop.
  • Timing instructions differ by product, so follow the package directions or ask a healthcare professional.
i

There is no single best pill for everyone. A healthcare professional can help choose an option based on medical history, other medicines, side effects, and personal preferences. Availability and product guidance can vary by country.
