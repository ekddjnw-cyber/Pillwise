Frequently asked questions

A starting point for common questions. Always check the instructions for your specific pill.

1. Do contraceptive pills cause infertility?

Contraceptive pills do not generally cause long-term infertility. Fertility usually returns after stopping, although the time it takes for a person's usual cycle to return can vary.

2. Do I need to take the pill every day?

Most oral contraceptive pills need to be taken daily. Timing requirements vary by product, especially for progestin-only pills. Follow the leaflet and advice from your healthcare professional.

3. Will the pill protect me from STIs?

No. Contraceptive pills help prevent pregnancy but do not protect against sexually transmitted infections. Condoms can reduce the risk of many STIs.

4. What if I miss a pill?

The right steps depend on the pill type, how many pills were missed, and where you are in the pack. Check the patient leaflet or contact a pharmacist or healthcare professional promptly; emergency contraception may be relevant in some situations.

5. Do side effects always happen?

No. Some people have side effects, some have only mild or temporary changes, and others notice none. If symptoms are persistent, severe, or worrying, seek professional advice.

6. Can anyone take contraceptive pills?

No single method is suitable for everyone. Certain health conditions, medicines, and risk factors can affect which pill is appropriate. A healthcare professional can help assess this.

7. When should I get medical help?

Seek urgent help for symptoms such as sudden chest pain, trouble breathing, sudden neurological symptoms, or severe unusual headache. See the side effects page for more warning signs.
